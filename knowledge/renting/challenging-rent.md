## How to challenge a rent increase

Renters can ask Consumer Affairs Victoria to investigate and report on rent increases if they believe the increase is higher than the market range.

This is called a rent assessment or a rent increase investigation. It is a free service.

Renters can’t be forced to leave a property because they ask for a rent assessment.

If you are renting a property and you believe the new rent is higher than the market range, you should try to negotiate with your rental provider (landlord). If you can’t agree about the new rent amount, you can ask Consumer Affairs Victoria to investigate and report on it. It will decide if the new rent amount is too high by working out the market rental value of the property.

### Before you request a rent assessment

You can compare the proposed rent with similar properties in your area. This can help you decide whether the increase reflects local market conditions and may help you discuss the increase with your rental provider.

You can also use the Service Victoria Rent Increase Calculator to compare the proposed increase with the Consumer Price Index (CPI) for Melbourne. Market rent and CPI are only some of the factors considered when assessing whether an increase is excessive.

## Challenging high rent

You can only ask for a rent assessment where the rent has not increased but you still think it is too high if the rental provider has reduced or removed services, facilities or other items that were previously part of the rental agreement, but hasn’t reduced the rent.

## How to get a rent assessment

You must ask for an assessment within 30 days of your rental provider giving you the written notice to tell you about the rent increase.

Step 1: Use the Request for rental assessment form to ask Consumer Affairs Victoria for a rent assessment.

Step 2: A case officer will contact you. An inspector may need to visit the property. The inspector writes a report on behalf of the Director of Consumer Affairs Victoria and sends copies to you and your rental provider. You can use this report to apply to Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria (RDRV).

Step 3: If the rent assessment says that the increase is too high, and your rental provider won’t agree to lower the rent, you can use the assessment to go to RDRV. If the dispute is not resolved by RDRV, they will help you apply to VCAT. You must apply to RDRV within 30 days of getting the rent assessment report.

VCAT can set a maximum rent. For private rental properties this maximum rent will usually be set for 12 months.

## What is assessed

From 31 March 2026, Consumer Affairs Victoria and VCAT have additional considerations when assessing rent increases. The factors set out in the law and regulations include:

- the rent payable for comparable rented premises in the same locality
- the proposed rent compared to the current rent
- the rate of increase compared with the Consumer Price Index (CPI) for Melbourne
- the number, amount and timing of any rent increases in the preceding 24 months
- the state of repair and general condition of the premises
- the location, land size, facilities and general condition of the property and comparable rented properties
- any changes in the rent and condition of the premises or facilities since the start of the rental agreement or the last rent increase
- any valuation of the premises
- if an infringement notice has been issued or an order made by VCAT or a court for a relevant breach of the rental laws.
