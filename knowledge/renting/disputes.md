## Steps to resolving a renting dispute

Disputes can often be settled without going to a hearing at VCAT. The steps are:

- know your rights and responsibilities
- talk to your agent or rental provider
- contact Consumer Affairs Victoria
- if still unresolved, apply to Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria (RDRV), which will help you go to the Victorian Civil and Administrative Tribunal (VCAT) if needed.

For illegal evictions, or urgent repairs where the rental provider or property manager does not respond quickly, contact Consumer Affairs Victoria straight away.

## Talking about disputes and putting it in writing

Try talking about the issue with the other person: explain the problem, say how you would like it resolved, and say what obligations you think they are not meeting. If you agree how to solve the problem, record this in writing.

If you cannot agree, you can put the complaint in writing using a standard notice. Renters can use it to tell rental providers that non-urgent repairs are required, that they paid for urgent repairs and need to be repaid, or that they paid utility charges that are not their responsibility.

When someone breaks the rental agreement or law in a way that is a breach of duty, either the renter or the rental provider can send a breach of duty notice. This can be the first step in ending a rental agreement.

## Going to RDRV

RDRV is a free alternative dispute resolution service offered by VCAT. It’s available to renters, rental providers and estate agents to resolve disputes about many renting issues, including rent increases, repairs and bonds. You can contact RDRV by phone, online or in person at any VCAT office.

Each application is case managed by a resolution coordinator, who liaises between you and your rental provider to try to reach a resolution early. Methods may include facilitated discussions and mediation.

## Going to VCAT

If the case isn’t resolved at RDRV, they can help you go to VCAT. You will need to provide evidence for your case, so keep letters, emails, records of phone calls, evidence of losses, photos or videos, and receipts if you are asking to be repaid for something.

VCAT prioritises cases that the renting laws say must be heard within a certain time, and those considered urgent.

## Further help

Renters in the private rental market who are financially disadvantaged or experiencing family violence can get help with dispute resolution from the Tenancy Assistance and Advocacy Program.

Dear Landlord is a free self-help tool for renters facing eviction for being behind in rent. It can help draft a payment plan request, prepare a VCAT review application, and find financial or legal help.

Renters aged 55 years or more can get advice and support from HAAG (Housing for the Aged Action Group).

Consumer Affairs Victoria cannot help with disputes between renters; contact the Dispute Settlement Centre of Victoria instead.
