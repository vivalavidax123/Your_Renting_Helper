## Right of entry and reasons for entry

Rental providers (landlords) and their agents have the right to enter a property, but there are rules about the reasons they can enter and when they can enter. Renters must let rental providers and agents enter if they are legally allowed and have given proper notice. Entering the property includes the garden or outside of the property, not just the inside.

A rental provider or their agent can only enter the property for one of these reasons:

- a general (routine) inspection to make sure the property is in good condition
- making repairs or doing something else the law says the rental provider must do
- showing the property to people who might rent or buy it, or to lenders
- having the property valued
- taking photos or videos to advertise the property
- having reasonable grounds to believe the renter has broken their obligations, for example damaged the property
- inspecting the property for VCAT family violence proceedings.

## Notice required before entry

The notice must be in writing and state the reason for entry. Minimum notice depends on the reason:

- general (routine) inspection: 7 days
- repairs or other legal responsibilities: 24 hours
- showing the property to renters, buyers or lenders: 48 hours
- having the property valued: 7 days
- taking photos or making videos for advertising: 7 days
- believing the renter has broken their obligations: 24 hours
- family violence proceedings in VCAT: 24 hours

The notice must be delivered by post (allowing enough time for delivery) or personally to the renter between 8 am and 6 pm.

## When entry is allowed

The rental provider or their agent can only enter the property between 8 am and 6 pm on any day except a public holiday. Entry outside these times is only possible if the renter agrees, and that agreement must be made no more than 7 days before the entry. If the renter has good reasons to limit the times the rental provider can enter, they can apply to VCAT to order these limits.

## Renters’ rights to refuse entry

A renter can refuse entry if:

- the visit isn’t between 8 am and 6 pm, or it’s a public holiday
- they haven’t been given written notice
- the notice wasn’t given properly (for example not far enough in advance)
- the rental provider wants to show the property to prospective renters after giving a notice to vacate, but it’s before the last 21 days of the rental agreement
- the rental provider wants to do a general inspection, but one has already been done in the last 6 months.

## Rules during entry

The rental provider must not exercise their right to enter in an unreasonable manner, or stay (or let others stay) longer than necessary, unless the renter agrees. If a renter thinks the rental provider is acting unreasonably, they can apply to VCAT, which can make a compensation or prohibition order, or stop the rental provider entering for a set period.

The rental provider can enter if the renter is not home, if valid written notice has been given or the renter agreed. The renter is not required to leave the property during an inspection or showing.

The right to enter includes anyone needed for the purpose of the visit, such as a valuer, photographer or tradesperson.

If something belonging to the renter is damaged or taken during entry, the renter may apply to VCAT for compensation.

## General inspections

A general inspection may only be made after the first 3 months of the rental agreement, and at most every 6 months. The rental provider can inspect all of the property.

## Showing the property to prospective renters

The property can be shown to prospective renters if the renter has given a notice of intention to vacate, or the rental provider has given a notice to vacate. Open inspections must happen no more than 21 days before the end of the rental agreement. The property must not be shown more than twice a week, or for longer than one hour each time, unless the renter agrees otherwise.

A renter who is a protected person under family violence or personal safety law can require showings by appointment only, rather than open inspections.

## Showing the property to buyers and sales inspection compensation

To show the property to potential buyers, the renter must have been told the owner is going to sell at least 14 days before, using the Notice of intention to sell the property form. The rental provider or agent must make reasonable efforts to agree days and times with the renter. Showings are limited to twice a week, for no longer than one hour each time, unless the renter agrees otherwise.

Rental providers must compensate renters for each sales inspection. The compensation is either half a day’s rent or $30, whichever is greater. For example, with a weekly rent of $250 the compensation is $30 per inspection, because half a day’s rent is only about $18. With a weekly rent of $900 the compensation is $64 per inspection.

## Photos and videos for advertising

The renter may object in writing if photos or video for advertising would show belongings that could identify them or reveal sensitive information, are valuable and would increase the risk of theft, are unreasonable to remove or hide, or could identify someone at risk of family or personal violence. If the renter objects, the rental provider must not take photos or videos that do any of these things, and the renter may ask to see the material before it is used.

A photo or video not originally produced for advertising can only be used for advertising if the renter agrees in writing.
