## Rental properties must meet the minimum standards

There are 15 categories of minimum standards for rental properties. All rental properties must meet the standards for each category.

Rental providers (landlords) and agents must make sure a property meets minimum standards at the time they advertise or offer to let the property, and before the renter moves in.

If a property does not meet the minimum standards, this counts as an urgent repair.

## When minimum standards apply

For existing rental agreements, the minimum standards (Schedule 4 of the Residential Tenancies Regulations 2021) apply if your agreement started on or after 29 March 2021, or started before 29 March 2021 and became a periodic (month to month) agreement on or after 29 March 2021. They don't apply if your rental agreement became a periodic agreement before 29 March 2021.

From 13 October 2026, all rental providers must keep records sufficient to show the property met the minimum standards before it was advertised or offered for rent, and before the renter moved in. Consumer Affairs Victoria can ask to see these records.

New minimum rental standards for energy efficiency will come into effect from 1 March 2027.

## Bathrooms, kitchen, laundry and toilets

A bathroom must have a washbasin and a shower or bath and be connected to a reasonable supply of hot and cold water. Showers must have a shower head with a 3-star water efficiency rating (1- or 2-star is acceptable if a 3-star one cannot be installed).

The property must have a kitchen with a dedicated cooking and food preparation area, a sink in good working order connected to a reasonable supply of hot and cold water, and a stovetop in good working order that has two or more burners. If there is an oven, it needs to be in good working order.

If there is a laundry on the property, it must be connected to a reasonable supply of hot and cold water.

The toilet must be in good working order, connected to sewerage or an approved wastewater system, and be in a separate room or an appropriate room like a bathroom.

## Electrical safety

Rental properties must have modern switchboards, with circuit breakers and electrical safety switches (residual current devices) installed. Rental providers are responsible for engaging an electrician to ensure the property complies.

## Heating

All rental properties must have a fixed heater (not portable) in good working order in the main living area.

For rental agreements entered into from 29 March 2023, this must be an energy efficient fixed heater: a non-ducted air conditioner or heat pump with a 2 star or above energy rating, a gas space heater with a 2 star or above energy rating, a ducted or hydronic heating system with an outlet in the main living area, or a solid fuel appliance such as a fireplace or wood burning stove. If there is an existing fixed heater that is not energy efficient, the rental provider must upgrade it.

In some apartment blocks it may be unreasonable to install an energy efficient heater, for example because of owners corporation rules or very high installation cost. The rental provider must still install a fixed heater in the main living area, should have evidence that the energy efficient option is unreasonable, and must tell the renter before they enter into a rental agreement.

## Lighting, ventilation, mould and structure

Inside rooms, corridors and hallways must have access to light. At night, renters should have access to artificial light.

Rental properties must have adequate ventilation in all habitable rooms including the bathroom, shower, toilet and laundry.

All rooms must be free from mould and damp caused by or related to the building structure.

The property must be structurally sound and weatherproof.

## Locks, windows and window coverings

External entry doors must have functioning deadlocks, or locks that can be unlocked with a key from the outside and with or without a key from the inside, with some exceptions (for example a screen door, or a door behind another locked security barrier).

All external windows that can open must have a functioning latch to secure them against external entry, and must be able to be left open or closed.

Windows in rooms likely to be used as bedrooms or living areas must be fitted with curtains or blinds that can be closed, block light and provide privacy.

From 1 December 2025, all corded internal window coverings, such as blinds and curtains, must have an anchor installed to secure the cords and prevent them forming loops.

## Bins

Rental providers must supply a rubbish bin and a recycling bin for the renter to use. The bins must be vermin proof and meet council collection standards.
