## Changes from 13 October 2026

- Strengthened bond claims: rental providers must notify the renter in advance if they will claim on the bond at the end of the rental agreement, and must provide evidence to support the claim.
- Records for minimum standards: rental providers or their agents must keep records showing the property met the rental minimum standards before it was advertised, offered, or the renter moved in, and give them to Consumer Affairs Victoria if asked.
- Ban on application and rent payment fees: rental providers and agents cannot charge renters fees for rental applications or rent payments. This adds to the ban on third party businesses (such as online rental platforms) charging such fees, which started on 31 March 2026.
- Gas and electrical safety checks every 2 years for all rental properties: rental providers must arrange checks by a qualified tradesperson every two years. Gas checks are only needed where there are gas appliances, fixtures or fittings. If a renter on an agreement from before March 2021 doesn’t know when the last checks were done, they can ask the rental provider in writing for the date. If no check was done in the 2 years before the renter moves in, the rental provider must arrange one as soon as possible.
- Gas safety check before draughtproofing: if the property has gas, a licensed or registered gasfitter must do a gas safety check within 6 months before any draughtproofing work begins.

## Changes from 9 September 2026

Renters can pay their bond directly to the RTBA. A renter, by written notice to the rental provider, may elect to pay their bond directly to the RTBA. The rental provider must then choose this option when they start the bond lodgement. Renters must pay within 2 business days after receiving the bond request from the RTBA.

## Changes from 31 March 2026

- New standard form for rental applications: rental providers and agents must use the prescribed rental application form.
- Rules for information that can be asked of rental applicants: limited to what is necessary to assess suitability, confirm identity and confirm capacity to pay the advertised rent.
- Ban on third party fees for applications and rent payments.
- Additional considerations for rent increase investigations by Consumer Affairs Victoria and VCAT, including RDRV.

## Changes from 1 December 2025

All rental properties must have secured cords on internal window coverings (blind cord safety anchors) to prevent them forming loops.

## Changes from 25 November 2025

- Ban on no fault evictions: rental providers cannot issue a renter with a notice to vacate without a valid reason, even at the end of a fixed-term agreement. When a fixed-term agreement ends, it automatically becomes a periodic (month-by-month) lease unless a new fixed-term agreement is signed or the rental provider issues a notice to vacate for a valid reason. Other reasons, such as sale of the property, renovation, or failure to pay rent, are still valid.
- Longer notice periods: rental providers must give 90 days' notice of a rent increase and certain notices to vacate. Shorter notice periods still apply when a renter is at fault.
- Ban on all types of rental bidding: agents and rental providers cannot accept offers to pay rent higher than the advertised amount, or more than one month’s rent in advance.
- Rental properties must meet minimum standards when advertised, not just before a renter moves in. Advertising a property that does not meet them is an offence.
- Annual smoke alarm safety checks for all rental agreements, whenever they started.
- Rules to protect renters’ personal information from rental applications, including destruction and de-identification. Disclosing it without consent is an offence.

## Upcoming changes

- Phased in from 1 March 2027: new minimum energy efficiency standards for heating, cooling, hot water, showerheads, ceiling insulation and draughtproofing.
- From 1 June 2027: a rental provider cannot refuse a renter who wants to pay their bond directly to the RTBA.
- From 1 June 2027: rental providers and agents must not unreasonably refuse a request for an additional key or fob (they may charge a reasonable fee), and must not deactivate a key or fob unless permitted.
- From 1 June 2027: a cap on lease break fees when a renter ends a fixed-term agreement early.
- From 1 June 2027: a rental provider must not refuse to let premises because of family violence or personal violence.
