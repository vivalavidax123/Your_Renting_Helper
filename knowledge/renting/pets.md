## Asking to keep a pet

Renters who want to have a pet in the property must ask their rental provider (landlord) for permission. Rental providers must have a good reason to refuse the renter’s request. Rental providers can apply to VCAT for an order to refuse permission.

A pet means any animal except an assistance dog, which is a dog trained to help a person with a disability. Assistance dogs do not count as pets, and people with assistance dogs cannot be discriminated against.

Renters who want to keep a pet should complete a Pet request form (a separate form for each pet), give it to their rental provider, and keep a copy.

The rental provider has 14 days (starting the day after they receive the form) to make a decision.

- If they agree to the renter having the pet, they should give consent in writing.
- If they don’t agree, they should apply to VCAT and tell the renter they have done so within the 14 days. The renter can’t keep a pet while VCAT is making a decision.
- If the rental provider does not respond at all within the 14 days the renter can keep the pet.

It is a good idea for renters to give information that will help the rental provider decide, such as the pet’s age, temperament and training, references from a vet, trainer or previous rental provider, and why the property is suitable. Renters should also check that the pet complies with local council laws.

## Conditions and VCAT’s role

The rental provider can try to negotiate conditions for keeping a pet, for example that the pet is not allowed inside. If the renter does not agree to the conditions and the rental provider wants to exclude the pet, they must apply to VCAT. Any agreed conditions should be put in writing.

When deciding, VCAT may consider the type of pet, the type of property, its appliances, fixtures and fittings, other relevant laws such as local council laws, and anything else it considers relevant.

If VCAT makes an order excluding the pet from the property, the renter has 14 days to comply with the order. If the renter has not complied after that, the rental provider can give them a notice to vacate, with a minimum of 28 days’ notice.

## Pets causing damage or problems

All renters, including those with pets, must take care to avoid damaging the property, keep it reasonably clean, and not cause a nuisance to neighbours.

If the renter does not meet these duties, the rental provider can give them a Notice of breach of duty, telling the renter to fix the breach or pay for any damage. This could apply to damage caused by a pet. If the renter does not comply, the rental provider can apply to VCAT for a compensation or compliance order. VCAT will consider fair wear and tear and the age and condition of the damaged items.

If a rental provider reasonably believes a renter is keeping a pet without consent, they can apply to VCAT for an order to exclude the pet.

## Pet bonds

Rental providers and owners cannot ask for an additional bond as a ‘pet bond’.

## Pets in apartments and units

An owners corporation may have its own rules about pets in common areas, which the renter must follow. Rental providers should give the renter a copy of the owners corporation rules when they move in. If the owners corporation decides the pet is dangerous or is causing a nuisance, the renter may have to remove the pet.
