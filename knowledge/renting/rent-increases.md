## Rules for increasing rent

From 25 November 2025, the minimum notice period for rental providers to issue a rent increase notice changed from 60 to 90 days.

A rental provider (landlord) can increase the rent for their property, but there are rules about what they can do.

The rental provider is not allowed to increase the rent during a fixed term agreement unless the rental agreement (lease) has an additional clause (term) allowing for an increase. If this is the case, the agreement must also state how the increase will be calculated.

To put the rent up, the rental provider must give the renter (tenant) a Notice of proposed rent increase via the method agreed to in the rental agreement at least 90 days in advance.

Important:

- If the rent increase rules are not followed the increase may be invalid.
- Renters can challenge a rent increase if they think it is higher than the market range.
- There are also limits on how often the rent can be increased.

## How much rent can increase

The law doesn’t say exactly how much a rental provider can put the rent up by.

However, the law does require a rental provider to give their renter information about how they have calculated the rent increase. The rental provider must inform the renter using a Notice of rent increase form. This form must be given to the renter at least 90 days before the increase is due to start.

In practice this means a rental provider must show that:

- the Notice of rent increase provides for a specific amount of rent increase, and
- the increase has been calculated in line with the method outlined in the Notice of rent increase.

The 4 most common methods for calculating a rent increase are:

- according to how much prices are generally changing in the economy, known as the consumer price index (CPI)
- according to average rent prices in Victoria, known as the metropolitan rent index or the regional rent index.
- by a fixed percentage increase – a percentage should be specified (e.g. 3%)
- by a fixed dollar amount – a dollar amount should be specified (e.g. $10)

However, rental providers can use other approaches if they prefer. Simply referring to a method in the rental agreement is not enough.

For example, the rental agreement may say that rent will be increased by $5 every 12 months. This means the rental provider can increase the rent by $5 per year by issuing the renter with a Notice of rent increase form.

### Examples of invalid and valid wording

Invalid wording (invalid increase): "Rent increased by dollar amount", "Based on the rental market valuation", "As per rental provider’s instructions", "All properties in the same building are leased between (price range)", or just "CPI".

Valid wording (examples only): "The next rent increase will be by 1.8% in line with the Consumer Price Index increase as reported by the Australian Bureau of Statistics for the September 2025 quarter." or "The next rent increase will be by $20 in line with the statewide rent increase for the June 2025 quarter."

If you are a renter and you think the rent increase is invalid you can request a review.

## How often rent can increase

A rental provider can only increase the rent at certain times. How often depends on the type of rental agreement, and when the agreement was signed. In most cases, rent cannot be increased more than once every 12 months, even if the rental agreement specifies a rent increase before that time.

### Agreements (leases) shorter than 5 years

Rental agreements that have an end date are fixed-term agreements. Most go for 5 years or less.

Rental providers must wait until the end of the rental agreement to increase the rent, unless the agreement says otherwise, and only as often as the rules below allow.

If the agreement does say rent can be increased during the fixed term, there are rules about how often:

- for agreements that started before 19 June 2019, the rent can’t be increased more than once every 6 months
- for rental agreements that started on or after 19 June 2019, the rent can’t be increased more than once every 12 months

### Agreements (leases) longer than 5 years

In a long-term agreement (more than 5 years), rent must not be increased more than once every 12 months, and only if the agreement says that the rent can be increased.

### Periodic (month by month) agreements

Rental agreements that don’t have an end date are periodic rental agreements. They are often called month by month agreements. People usually start a periodic agreement when a fixed-term agreement ends – this happens automatically unless a new fixed-term agreement is agreed and signed.

In periodic agreements, there are still rules for how often the rent can be increased:

- for periodic rental agreements that started before 19 June 2019, the rent can’t be increased more than once every 6 months
- for rental agreements that became periodic on or after 19 June 2019, the rent can’t be increased more than once every 12 months.

This is true even if it has been month by month for several years. If the rental provider increases the rent, the renter can accept the increase, ask for a rent assessment or tell the rental provider they want to end the agreement and leave, known as issuing a notice of intention to vacate.

## Telling renters about rent increases

Rental providers must tell renters about rent increases at least 90 days before they are going to put the rent up, and they must use the Notice of rent increase form.

The rental provider must tell the renter:

- how much the rent will increase
- how the rental provider calculated the rent increase - if the rent is increased during a fixed term rental agreement, the rental agreement must indicate how the rent would be increased (for example, consumer price index, rent index, percentage, or fixed dollar amount)
- what the renter can do if they think the increase is too high.

Rent can’t be increased unless the rental provider has used the right form. If the rental provider doesn’t use the right form, the notice isn’t valid. The renter only has to pay the increase if they are given proper notice.

## Rent can’t be increased to penalise a renter

A rental provider can’t ask a renter to pay more rent because they have broken any part of the rental agreement. Rental agreements can’t include anything that says a renter has to pay more rent if they break any of the terms of the rental agreement.

There are financial penalties if the rental provider includes a prohibited term like this in the agreement, and such a term can’t be enforced.

## Changes to the bond when rent increases

Additional bond amounts can only be required for rent increases when there is a long-term agreement which is being renewed for another fixed term of more than 5 years.

The rental provider must give the renter 120 days written notice that they want them to ‘top up’ the bond using the Notice requesting additional bond. The renter must pay the additional bond amount within these 120 days. The rental provider can’t do this more than once every 5 years.

Additional bond amounts cannot be required for a periodic or fixed short-term agreement.
