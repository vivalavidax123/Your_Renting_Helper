## Giving notice to leave

Renters who want to move out can either talk to their rental provider (landlord) to reach an agreement, or give formal notice that they want to leave. A notice of intention to vacate is a formal statement that the renter wants to end the rental agreement (lease).

There are only certain reasons renters can end a rental agreement early without breaking the agreement and having to pay compensation or lease break fees.

## Notice at the end of an agreement

Renters must give 28 days' notice if they want to leave at the end of a rental agreement (lease).

Renters on fixed-term agreements, like a 12-month agreement, will automatically move to a month-to-month agreement if they do not give notice.

For fixed-term agreements, the end date on the notice of intention to vacate cannot be before the end date of the rental agreement. If it is earlier, the renter is breaking the agreement and may have to pay lease break fees.

If the renter has not moved in yet and the property does not meet the rental minimum standards, the renter can give a notice of termination and the agreement ends immediately.

## Reasons and notice periods for leaving early

The renter must give written notice for any of these reasons:

- the property becomes unfit to live in or is destroyed to the extent that it is unsafe: immediate
- the rental provider has breached a VCAT compliance or compensation order: 14 days
- the rental provider has breached a duty for the third time after two breach of duty notices: 14 days
- the rental provider refused changes needed to make the property suitable for someone with disability: 14 days
- the renter needs temporary crisis accommodation, special and personal care, or is moving into social housing (evidence required): 14 days
- the renter was given a Notice of intention to sell, unless the sale was disclosed before the agreement started: 14 days
- the rental provider gave the renter a notice to vacate (for example for major repairs, demolition, sale, business use, moving in, or ending a fixed term): 14 days, although in some cases the rental provider may seek some costs
- a long-term agreement was verbal or didn’t use the right form: 28 days
- any other reason: 28 days.

If renters give notice for any other reason before the end of the rental agreement, they will be breaking the rental agreement and may have to pay costs.

A renter may ask a rental provider to leave without formal notice. The rental provider does not have to agree. If they do, the agreement should be recorded in writing.

People experiencing family violence who need to change or end their rental agreement to be safe can apply to VCAT.

## How to give notice

The renter must put the notice in writing. Using the Notice of intention to vacate rented premises by renter form is recommended but not compulsory. It can be delivered to the rental provider by post, electronic communication (such as email), or by hand.

## Withdrawing a notice or not leaving

If you have given notice but want to stay, tell your rental provider as soon as possible, give written notice withdrawing your intention to vacate, and seek the rental provider’s agreement in writing. If the rental provider does not agree, the renter can apply to VCAT. Unless VCAT says otherwise, the renter must leave by the date on the original notice.

If a renter gives notice but does not leave, the rental provider can apply to VCAT for an order for the renter to move out.
